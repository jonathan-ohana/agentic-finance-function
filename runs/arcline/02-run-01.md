# 87 — Arcline run 01: the first blind test

*25 Aug 2026. Two agents, clean contexts, sealed folder. 17 of 25 planted defects found and
quantified, 4 missed, and — the finding that matters — **four of the eight non-hits were defects in
the dataset, not in the agents.** Outputs in `Arcline-Finance/_runs/run-01/`.*

---

## The protocol, and why it had to be this

I built the instance and I know the answer key. Anything I produce myself is a demonstration, not a
test. So: a **sealed working copy** with `99-answer-key/` and `_generator/` physically removed, two
subagents in clean contexts, neither told what to look for beyond the shape of a good close, and
scoring done afterwards by me against the key.

Physically removing the key matters more than instructing around it. Doc 74 §1 makes the general
version of this argument about tool surfaces: *a prompt asks a model to decline; a surface with no
function for the wrong answer does not have to ask.* The same applies to a directory.

**Agent A — variance analysis** (CL-31): explain January against the plan of record, at driver
level, with evidence.
**Agent B — accrual prep** (CL-10, 11, 16, 18, 19, 33): what to accrue, which reversals are missing,
what the post-close inbox implies, plus a delegation-of-authority pass over the January entries.

### What they got, and how

The five highest-value plants were all found and correctly quantified: the USD 215,000 unsupported
reclass, the unreversed Anthropic accrual, the training GPU in cost of revenue, the duplicate

Three things go beyond finding the plant:

**The subtotal trap worked as designed and was defeated.** Total opex is 0.2% off plan. Agent A
opened it anyway and found R&D +180.6k against S&M −201k, almost all of it one entry. It then
went further than the plant: the reclass drives account 7040 to a **credit balance of

**One agent refused to post.** Agent B declined the USD 92,000 SLA credit because "Kestrel Foods"
appears in no customer file, and said so rather than booking a plausible number. That is the

**Cross-checking outside the ledger.** Agent A checked the CoreWeave invoice against the CoreWeave
contract and noted the agreement is rolling monthly and metered, with no reserved capacity in it —
so the invoice is questionable on its own terms, before the classification question.

### What they missed, and why it splits in two

## Four defects in the dataset, exposed by the run

This is the more valuable half of the result.

**1. Split customer identity.** `plants.apply()` renames five customers, but it runs after the
change log, the usage file and the AR subledger have been built. So `ARC-C-0112` is *Northgate
Insurance* in the agreements file and the order form, and *Jasper Insurance* in usage, invoices and
the change log. Same ID, two names. Agent A handled it correctly by keying on ID — which is why it
still found PL-07 — but the data is wrong and a weaker engine would have split the customer in two.
**Fix: apply the renames before anything downstream is built.**

**2. PL-20 was planted on the wrong customer.** The key claims USD 38,000 of lost January usage
revenue, USD 456,000 annualised. `ARC-C-0027` is a small account whose January overage is **zero** —
it fell below its allowance entirely. The real impact is USD 1,472. Agent A found the account,
quoted the file note, ranked it eighth and dismissed it as part of a broad 4.1% volume decline —
**and was right to.** The plant failed, not the agent. **Fix: move it to a top-five usage account
(ARC-C-0043 carries 31,278 of January overage) and size the key from the data.**

**3. PL-05 has no evidence trail.** The Meridian amendment exists only in the order-form markdown.
There is no row in `amendments.csv` and none in `customer_mrr_changes.csv`, so there is nothing to
join invoices against and the route the key describes does not exist. **Undiscoverable as built.**

**4. PL-06 is not distinguishable.** `notice_deadline` is computed for every contract in the file,
so nothing marks this one out; with `renewals_to_date = 0` and a term ending 31 January it reads as
a contract simply ending. **Fix: give the renewal watch something to detect — a deadline that has
passed with the term still running.**

**And three amounts in the key are wrong**, because I asserted them in narrative instead of
computing them: PL-03 (48,802 → the ledger says **48,430.71**), PL-16 (184,000 → the plan
calculation computes **23,000.72**), PL-18 (~73,000 loss → the balance computes to **49,475**, and
it is a **gain**). In all three the agent's number is the true one.

There is a lesson in this and it is not a small one. **The answer key was written the way a plan is
written — by typing the number beside the thing instead of deriving it from the thing.** Doc 85
records the same failure in the LRP verifier: a test that passes on the wrong number is worse than
no test, because it is counted. Here the failure ran the other way: three tests that would have
*failed* a correct answer. The key must be generated from the data, like every other artefact.

## Two further data bugs the agents surfaced on their own

**Preparer and approver names are not in the headcount register.** S. Patel, M. Halloran and
T. Byrne appear on several hundred entries and on no roster. Agent A flagged it as a data-integrity
problem. It is one.

**The prepaid release schedule steps by an identical factor within each function on 1 January** —
×1.640 for all ten S&M vendors, ×1.370 for all eight R&D — with one renewal in the month. That is a
generator artefact of setting January targets independently of the FY25 shape, and it ties to no
agreement. Agent B refused to sign CL-19 over it.

Also, correctly: **the CL-01…CL-29 evidence workbooks the checklist names do not exist anywhere in
the folder**, and CL-14 is marked Complete over a blank payroll reconciliation. Both true.

## What this says about the workflow

1. **Driver-level explanation works when the evidence is in the ledger.** Every plant with a journal
   entry, a document reference and a memo behind it was found. The memo field did most of the work —
   "model training cluster" is what made PL-04 findable.
2. **Contract-to-ledger reconciliation works only where both sides are in structured data.** PL-07
   was found because the ramp is in `customer_agreements.csv`. PL-05 was missed because the
   amendment is only in prose. That is the boundary, and it is worth drawing deliberately: an
   instance should carry plants on both sides of it, and the ones in prose should be *known* to be
   the hard case rather than accidentally impossible.
3. **The close loop has no vendor lens.** Two of four genuine misses are spend-review findings. A
   monthly close will not grow that lens on its own.
4. **Both agents refused to sign.** Agent A: do not sign CL-34, seven blockers. Agent B: CL-19
   should not be signed. Neither was asked to opine on the sign-off.
